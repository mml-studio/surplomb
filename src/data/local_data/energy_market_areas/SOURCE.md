# Neighboring market outlines — provenance & license

`market_areas.geojson` gives the **Electricity mix** layer (*Mix électrique*,
`src/data/franceEnergy.js`) an outline to draw around each of the five market
zones with which éCO2mix publishes a commercial balance. RTE publishes no
geometry: `ech_comm_angleterre`, `ech_comm_espagne`, `ech_comm_italie`,
`ech_comm_suisse` and `ech_comm_allemagne_belgique` are five numbers and a
country name. So the outline comes from elsewhere.

## What the file contains, and what it does not

Generated by `scripts/build-energy-market-areas.mjs`, which prints the before /
after count on every run. Last run: **2,281 → 674 points, 70% lighter,
12,392 bytes.**

One feature per `ech_comm_*` field — so **four countries and one pair**:
`allemagne_belgique` carries Germany and Belgium in a single feature, because
éCO2mix publishes one balance for the two, and outlines labeled separately
would invent a split the data does not carry.

Removed on purpose, and for reasons that are not only about bytes:

- **Northern Ireland**, absent from the British outline. The counterpart of
  `ech_comm_angleterre` is the GB market zone; Northern Ireland trades in the
  all-island SEM with the Republic and is not part of it.
- **The Balearic and Canary Islands**, absent from the Spanish outline. They
  are island systems of their own, not the peninsular market against which
  `ech_comm_espagne` settles. The file draws the Spanish **market**, not the
  Spanish **state**.
- **Small islands** (Shetland, Anglesey, Rügen): under the 0.5 square-degree
  threshold, invisible at the altitude at which the layer is read.

Sicily and Sardinia are **kept**: they are two zones of the Italian market,
and `ech_comm_italie` settles against its aggregate.

## What the outline is not

It is **not** a map of interconnectors. The outline bounds the market zone; it
says nothing about where the cables cross the border. The arrow that leaves
the French coast starts from the **point of the French outline closest to the
market's reference point** — a computed geometric fact, not a converter
station.
